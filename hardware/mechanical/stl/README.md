# Print files (v0.3, ready to test)

One unit per blind, press-fit between the rail walls: the tilter (factory worm + worm wheel, N20 drive) with the AS5600
angle sensor built in. A 20T sensor gear meshes with the back of the worm wheel; the board mounts on the lid. The ESP32
and motor drivers live in a separate controller unit (one per 3 blinds).

Generated by [`fusion/TiltParts`](../fusion/TiltParts/TiltParts.py) and checked with
[`tools/fusion_verify.py`](../../../tools/fusion_verify.py) in Fusion before export:
- **Interference:** none between any printed part, the factory parts, the motor, the rod, the board or the rail.
- **Tilter gears:** zero overlap at mid-backlash, 6.9° backlash, no contact through a full adapter revolution (16 positions).
- **Meshes:** every STL is closed (0 non-manifold edges) and matches its Fusion body.
- **Printability** ([`tools/fusion_wallcheck.py`](../../../tools/fusion_wallcheck.py)): every wall of both case halves
  is measured from every pocket, bore and hole. Nothing is under 0.56 mm (the slicer needs ≥ 0.4 mm to lay a line).
  The thin ones are around the pinion's pocket, which is centred on the pinion with 0.4 mm of room all round: 0.59 mm of
  floor under it and a 0.56 mm divider to the adapter collar. The skin beside the adapter gear and collar is 0.7 mm,
  fully enclosed so the adapter can't move. Walls around screw, pilot, insert and peg holes are ≥ 1.6 mm, so a screw or
  insert can't bulge into a gear pocket.

**Printing the tilter halves:** both print **on their backs**, outer face on the bed and split face up. The worm groove,
bearing seats and gear pockets are then the top of the print: crisp and true, with no first-layer squish on surfaces the
worm and gears run against, and no supports. The only overhang is the small notch-filling block at one corner (a ~2 mm
ledge). See [main half](../../docs/renders/print-case-right.png) and [lid](../../docs/renders/print-case-left.png).

**Motor mount:** there's no motor tower. The lid is 3 mm thicker (11.5 mm), and the N20 gearbox sits 4 mm deep in a
rectangular socket in its outer face, below the AS5600 board. That stops it rotating, and 1 × M1.6 screw holds its face
against the wall. The rest of the motor sticks out free inside the rail, on the side away from the tilt drums (on the
drum side it hit a tilt spindle). `motor_side` in `TiltParts.py` flips it back if a blind ever needs that.

**Settings:** PETG (cases), PETG or PLA+ (gears), 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.12 mm layers for the gears and 0.16–0.2 mm for
the rest, 4 walls, 30–40 % infill, **no supports**. 45° helical teeth print as a 45° overhang. The cases are
solid (no lightening pockets). The only bridges are short flat ceilings printed over the bed: the M2 counterbores
and standoff holes in the lid, the magnet-head seat, and the bottom of the N20 socket in the lid (~10 mm across).
They bridge fine as they are; no supports needed.

## Envelope
- **Tilter:** inside the 55.5 × 36.5 mm rail except a dome over the worm's top end, which rises 2.1 mm into the open slot
  between the rail's top lips (the window frame is 6.5 mm above). Length along the rod: case −11.5 to +8.3 mm around
  the tilter plane, with the motor sticking out a further 21 mm on the lid side, away from the tilt drums (to −32.5 mm).
  Nothing sticks out on the drum side.
