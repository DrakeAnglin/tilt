# Measuring your blinds

Do this on the **largest, heaviest** blind first; that one sizes the motor. Record results in the table at the bottom.

## Tools
- Luggage scale or kitchen scale (grams)
- A 10 cm lever: a strip of wood, a ruler, or a quick 3D-printed arm that fits the wand hook / tilt rod
- Tape, marker, phone camera

## 1. Count the tilt travel
1. Tilt the slats fully closed one way (e.g. slats down).
2. Put a tape flag on the wand. Count **wand turns** until fully closed the other way. Record it.
3. If you can open the headrail: mark the **tilt rod** and note how many degrees it rotates over the same travel.

## 2. Measure tilt torque
Torque = force × lever length. With a 10 cm lever, **grams read on the scale ÷ 100 = kg·cm**.

1. Attach the lever to the wand hook (Option A) **and**, if accessible, to the tilt rod with the tilter disengaged (Option B).
2. Hook the scale at the 10 cm point and pull **perpendicular to the lever**, slowly, until it turns.
3. Measure at several points through the travel: the peak is usually near **fully closed**, where the slats squeeze together. Record the **maximum**.
4. Repeat 3 times and take the highest reading.

## 3. Headrail internals (photos please)
Remove the blind from its brackets and photograph:
- Inside the headrail end-to-end (top view)
- The tilter (worm gear unit) and how it clips in
- The tilt rod's cross-section (round, D, hex, square? what size?)
- Cordless lift parts: spring motor unit(s), cord spools, any shaft connecting them (needed for [lift-bonus.md](lift-bonus.md))
- Measure: headrail inner width/height, length, distance from the tilter to the end cap, free space at each end

## 4. Lift (bonus)
- Blind width × drop, rough weight (bottom rail + slats)
- Force needed to push the bottom rail up and pull it down (luggage scale on the bottom rail center)
- Total travel distance (fully down to fully up)
